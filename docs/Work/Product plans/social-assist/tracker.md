## Read from their sites — 10 Oct 2026

Not opened. No card. These notes are from their public pages.

Nothing here suggests tomorrow’s post from a clinic. The desk is the same: a calendar, a queue, analytics of views. The differences are below.

### ContentStudio — [contentstudio.io](https://contentstudio.io/)

A calendar with drafts, approvals, and a queue, plus an AI chat that writes the caption, makes the image or video, and can fill weeks of the calendar from one prompt. It also pulls trending topics and RSS feeds as idea sources. Their site says a 7-day trial needs no card. Still do not start it. This is the autopilot plus video generation we left aside.

### SocialBee — [socialbee.com](https://socialbee.com/)

Posts sit in categories (educational, promotional, and so on). Each category has a weekly time. A category can re-post the same items forever, or share once. Their AI can draft the caption and propose the weekly times. The recycle is the different piece. Ours brings a winner back on a season, and the doctor chooses. Theirs can repeat the same post with nobody looking.

### Later — [later.com](https://later.com/)

An Instagram planner. You drag photos into a grid and see the feed before it publishes. AI writes a caption from a prompt and says it learns the tone of past posts. It also suggests a best time from follower activity, and it counts clicks on its link-in-bio. The grid preview and the link clicks are the differences. A click is still not a booking.

### PostPlanify — [for practices](https://postplanify.com/social-media-scheduler-for-medical-practices)

A scheduler aimed at a practice with staff. Every post can wait for a second person (compliance, manager) before it publishes. They also sell AI captions for health tips, a month planned in one sitting, and posting to many networks. The different piece is the second approver. A solo doctor is their own approver.

### ViralDashboard — [healthcare](https://viraldashboard.io/jv/industries/healthcare)

The same approval chain for a clinic group, plus an audit of who approved what. Their healthcare page says the tool does not store or transmit protected health information, and it is not a clinical system. Also a review inbox. Built for a marketing team, not one doctor.

### Hootsuite — [healthcare note](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-healthcare/)

Do not start the trial. The note says a person reviews every AI draft before it is public, and patient details never go into the writer. Their newer AI (Wisdom) claims it can say what to create next from social performance and trends. That is the nearest public claim to a next-post line. It reads likes, comments, and public conversation. It does not read a diary.

### Sprout Social — [sproutsocial.com](https://sproutsocial.com/)

The enterprise version of the same desk, plus listening across public conversation and an AI agent (Trellis) that answers questions about performance and competitors. Paid. Skip. Nothing here is the clinic.

### Apaya — [healthcare social](https://apaya.com/enterprise/industry/multi-location-healthcare-social-media-management)

A demo, not a free signup. Built for a group of clinics. It writes the posts, every draft waits for a reviewer, and a brand memory holds banned phrases and approved language. Their page says it is public marketing, not health records. A reviewer can send a caption back when it sounds like a treatment claim. They also offer a switch to publish with nobody looking. Keep the banned-phrase memory. Leave the hands-free switch and the second employee.
